# Route Choreography
Continues from: docs/active-spikes/interaction-polish.md

## Purpose
Make route changes physical. Shared elements (the case-study or writing photo plate and title plate) fly between card and detail; everything else on the outgoing page leaves by moving, and the incoming page arrives by moving. The generic full-page fade stays as the fallback for routes that have no designed sequence.

This spike was split out of the interaction-polish spike on 2026-09-23, when that spike closed. It owns transition motion only. Integration of the live halftone renderer lives in the performance spike (`docs/active-spikes/performance-optimization.live-halftone.md`). Motion work must not depend on the renderer, and renderer work must keep this spike's round-trip check green.
## The Contract In One Paragraph
Nothing that is visible should stay still and then vanish, and nothing should pop out of existence in a single frame, unless the flying clone covers exactly that area at that moment. Surroundings leave the frame completely before the page swaps. Card shells either fade out almost immediately on click or become the traveling plate. In designed sequences, text moves through slits (a stationary clip with moving opaque ink) and does not fade. `corepack pnpm check:transitions` checks this contract mechanically; human QA judges feel, easing and timing.

## Settled Motion Rules
Carried verbatim from the interaction spike. These were accepted in user QA across S33–S59.
### Latest Writing Surroundings
Extend the existing Home choreography to the bento writing cards. Move each sibling as one list item, preserving its internal depth/image/text treatment. Same-row cards leave left/right; same-column cards leave up/down. Diagonal and ambiguous overlaps compare center separation normalized by the combined card sizes, with vertical ties. Section furniture and other Home sections keep vertical flow. Reverse uses freshly measured destination geometry after scrolling, so responsive layouts remain authoritative. Writing staggers fit inside the existing departure hold; reduced motion skips choreography. Preserve the primary morph and do not animate both a parent section and its children.
### Writing Plates And Archive Slits
Writing title grounds need 15px of cream below the text during flight as well as at rest. Match detail and archive italic IBM Plex Mono titles. Long detail dates and abbreviated archive dates are distinct content, not a shared element: exit through their own clip before travel and enter after landing. Archive excerpts split at actual rendered line boundaries; each opaque line moves through a separate slit. Retain the accessible source text and native wrapping. Keep the existing primary image/title ownership overlap. Phone Media + Text does not need an oversized TOC clearing matte, which can paint over preceding text; desktop/tablet retain that behavior. The contextual navigation bar has no bottom grey rule.

Slit entrance must be visible after the shared plate leaves, not just technically running beneath it. Hold destination-only text clipped until the flight releases, then move opaque lines with a slight stagger. Archive rows retain physical clipping without opacity fade and wait for small eager thumbnail decode before their reveal; image failure must release the row. Reset article paragraph margins inside compact archive rows.
### Archive Physical Continuity
A slit is a stationary clip with moving opaque ink. Whole-row mask wipes do not substitute for that motion. The selected row finishes its extras before neighboring rows begin their part entrances; source extras fully leave before lifting. Returns ripple outward by row distance: C, then B+D together, then A+E, then F among visible rows. Rows above enter from the top, rows below from the bottom. Departures split horizontally: above-right and below-left. Year labels and blue rules join their associated row’s ring. Each row shell and its thumbnail/title/excerpt/date should reveal with a small part stagger, with later shells still travelling while earlier contents enter. The historical 80% return cue remains in the current source; the user's latest request is to compare a 60% cue in a separate experiment. Keep compact35ms part stagger independent from90ms return-ring spacing. Departures should pair equal row distances with a wider stagger, wait for actual exit effects before unmounting, and let the selected row become the traveling text plate when that continuity is implemented. Offscreen content must not prolong the visible wave. Date and author stagger symmetrically in both directions.

Writing titles may have materially different line counts between archive, Home and detail. Preserve real DOM text and the plate morph, but measure word positions at the two settled layouts and interpolate those positions instead of re-running balanced wrapping at every intermediate width. Case-study titles retain their existing machinery.
### Writing Flight Image Readiness
Archive thumbnails remain small, but their loaded `currentSrc` is not sufficient for a hero-sized flight. Warm a bounded set of visible heroes and prioritize actual pointer/focus intent. Choose a decoded responsive hero before lifting and keep that texture stable throughout the morph. A bounded click-time wait shares the source-exit preparation; failed or very slow images use the loaded thumbnail fallback rather than stranding navigation. Do not turn archive browsing into eager downloading of every full-size image. Cacheable static media is required for reliable reuse of a prepared URL.
### Case-Study Extras
On Home→case-study detail, the case-study number and body excerpt leave through slit motion, with the excerpt per rendered text row. The rotating star uses a spin-shrink; the user confirmed on 2026-09-23 that this works well. Coordinate these exits with preflight and clone ownership so the traveling plate does not conceal unfinished motion. Preserve accessible text, reduced motion and reverse entry.
### Accepted Defaults
Flights use the shared 200ms default (S46/S47). Writing Archive departures are horizontal (S45); the vertical variant stays as a diagnostic option. Timing lives in the Sass motion palette and is exported as CSS custom properties that JavaScript reads.
## How To Verify
`corepack pnpm check:transitions [--origin=http://127.0.0.1:3002] [--width=1512] [--height=945] [--journey=case-study,latest-writing,archive] [--json]` drives system Chrome headless over the DevTools pipe. It hovers and clicks a real card, walks Back, and reports:
- any painted element of the outgoing page that is still visible and has not moved when it disappears (`still-then-vanished`), or has moved but is still on screen (`exit-incomplete`);
- any element that goes from visible to gone in one frame without moving, counting only the pixels the flying clone does not cover (`popped`);
- any moving element hidden in place while still on screen with more than two frames of its scheduled motion left (`cut-short`): the page swap interrupted its exit. The snappy ease-in covers most of its distance in the last frame or two, so a swap landing inside that window counts as finishing;
- a transition that does not settle, a stranded clone layer, a destination heading that is not fully shown, a source card still concealed 3 s after Back, and page errors.

It measures visible area through clipping ancestors, so slit exits register as leaving rather than popping. Card shells are judged by the exact uncovered pixels, because a shell's border ring is the part a person sees vanish. It does not judge easing, timing feel, colour or the renderer. Run it against a fresh static build (`corepack pnpm generate:preview`, or `static:generate` plus `static:preview`) before asking for human QA, and add a journey to it before building each new route pair.

Prefer verifying in the real generated build. Injected prototype servers (the S-series) were useful for isolating renderer questions, but they fake media and scripting, so they cannot certify motion in the delivered site.
## Exploratory Route Pairs
The user, 2026-09-23: “we don't really have to do the full page fade for much. We have that as a back up but I think we can add proper fully physical animation ins and outs for most pages.” Carried verbatim from the interaction spike:

The user is interested in replacing many ordinary full-page fallback fades with physical exits and entrances. This is a design sketch for later exploration, not an instruction to implement all routes now. Keep the existing fade as a fallback for routes without a designed sequence, and preserve reduced-motion/accessibility behavior. Prototype each route pair separately with full content parity and check actual visible continuity before promoting any direction to a settled design.

- Home → About: Home material leaves in varied physical directions. The About heading (“about” / “I make things carefully and ask why”) enters subtly from below; body paragraph enters through a bottom-up slit; the portrait enters from offscreen right. About → Home reverses this sequence.
- Home → Writing Archive: Home content above the writing link exits up and content below exits down. “Filed under,” “Writing,” the latest year and latest post enter together through a left-to-right slit-like sequence, then later rows enter staggered. Explore later rows from right to left or alternating right/left. Writing Archive → Home: archive rows leave in a staggered downward movement; Home content returns from varied directions.
- Home → Side Projects: the large green Conway's Game of Life field expands to fill the screen while text and button exit upward through slits, perhaps with a slight stagger. The user is considering a stronger dark-green visual identity for the destination so the enlarged green field can transition seamlessly, then the Conway animation can fade into a calm dark page. The destination look and final handoff are unresolved design questions, not approved styling changes.
